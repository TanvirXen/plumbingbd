**Short answer:** add up the watts of everything you want running during load-shedding, divide by 0.7 to get the VA, and choose the next IPS size up. Then size the battery for how many hours you need. Our [IPS calculator](/tools/ips-load-calculator/) does this for you.

## Step 1: List what must run

Most families keep fans, a few lights, the Wi-Fi router and maybe the TV on backup.

| Appliance | Typical watts |
|---|---|
| Ceiling fan | 60–80 W |
| LED bulb | 9–15 W |
| LED tube light | 18–22 W |
| Wi-Fi router | 10–15 W |
| LED TV | 60–100 W |
| Laptop | 45–90 W |

Example: 3 fans (225 W) + 5 LED lights (60 W) + router (12 W) + TV (80 W) = **377 W**.

## Step 2: Convert to VA

IPS units are rated in VA. Divide watts by 0.7: 377 ÷ 0.7 ≈ 540 VA → choose a **650 VA** IPS (or 1000 VA if you plan to add more later).

## Step 3: Size the battery

Battery Ah ≈ (watts × hours) ÷ (battery volts × 0.75). For 377 W for 3 hours on a 12 V system: (377 × 3) ÷ 9 ≈ **125 Ah**.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting the fridge or a motor on a small IPS.** Motors need a much higher starting current.
- **No separate backup line.** A proper installation runs a separate line so only chosen fans and lights run on IPS — the battery lasts longer and the IPS isn't overloaded.
- **Battery in a closed cupboard.** Batteries need ventilation.
