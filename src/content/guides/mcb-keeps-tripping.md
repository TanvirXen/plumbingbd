**Short answer:** an MCB trips to protect your wiring. The usual causes are **overload** (too many heavy appliances on one line), a **short circuit**, an **earth fault** (often from damp walls), or a **weak or wrongly rated breaker**. Replacing the breaker without finding the cause rarely fixes it.

## The four common causes

| Cause | Typical sign | What it usually means |
|---|---|---|
| Overload | Trips when AC, geyser, iron or microwave run together | That line carries more current than it was designed for |
| Short circuit | Trips instantly, sometimes with a spark or burning smell | Live and neutral wires touching — a damaged cable, socket or appliance |
| Earth fault | RCCB trips, often in monsoon | Current leaking to earth — damp walls, wet sockets, faulty appliance |
| Faulty breaker | Trips at low load, feels hot | Old or poor-quality breaker, or a loose terminal |

## What you can safely check

1. **Unplug everything on that line**, reset the breaker, then plug appliances back one by one. If one appliance trips it every time, that appliance is the problem.
2. **Note when it trips.** Only when the AC starts? Only when it rains? That tells the electrician a lot.
3. **Feel the DB box cover** (don't open it). If it's hot, switch off the main and call for help — loose terminals are a fire risk.

Never tape a breaker in the ON position or replace it with a bigger rating "so it stops tripping". The breaker is matched to the cable; a bigger breaker lets the cable overheat.

## When to call an electrician

- It trips with a spark, smell or burnt mark
- The DB box or a socket feels hot
- It trips during rain (possible seepage into wiring)
- You've added an AC or geyser recently
