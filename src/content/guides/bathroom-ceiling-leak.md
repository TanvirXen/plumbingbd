**Short answer:** a wet or dripping bathroom ceiling almost always comes from the **bathroom directly above** — a leaking pipe joint, a cracked floor trap, a failed commode seal, or water seeping through the floor around the shower. The fix starts with finding the exact source, so only that spot is opened.

## The usual causes

1. **Floor trap or drain joint** — the most common. Water from the floor drain leaks around the trap into the slab.
2. **Commode outlet seal** — leaks every time the flush is used.
3. **Concealed water line joint** — leaks all the time, even when nobody uses the bathroom.
4. **Shower area and tile grout** — water soaks through cracked grout or a damaged waterproof layer.

**Quick test:** if the drip continues when the upstairs bathroom isn't used, it's likely a supply-line leak. If it only drips after showers or flushes, it's a drain, trap or seal.

## Who pays?

In most buildings the owner of the flat where the leak comes from is responsible for fixing it, and the building committee handles common pipes (risers, main drains). Finding the exact source first avoids arguments between neighbours.

## How we fix it

- Trace the source with flow and pressure tests
- Open only the needed tiles
- Replace the joint, trap or seal; re-test before closing
- Advise on waterproofing if the floor itself is the problem

## Don't ignore it

Seepage weakens plaster, grows mould and can reach electrical wiring — causing short circuits or tripping breakers. If you see damp near switches or lights, switch off that line and ask our electrical team to check it.
